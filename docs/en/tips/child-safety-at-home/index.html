How to Childproof Your Home: Child Safety Products Room by Room

Once your baby starts crawling or walking, the house becomes one big exploration zone: drawers that open, sockets at hand height, and stairs begging to be climbed. The good news is that most childproofing needs simple, affordable products and one easy first step: get down to your child's eye level and walk around the house. Anything their hand can reach needs a solution.

Priorities first

  • Anchor furniture and the TV: tall wardrobes, chests of drawers and TVs can tip over if a child climbs them. Fix them to the wall with anti-tip straps or brackets.
  • Medicines and cleaning products: in a high, locked cabinet — not under the sink.
  • Button batteries and magnets: very dangerous if swallowed; check that battery covers on remotes and toys are secure.
  • Blind and curtain cords: tie them up high, out of reach and away from the cot.

Room by room

  • Living room: corner guards for sharp table edges, covers for unused sockets, and cables off the floor.
  • Kitchen: drawer and cabinet locks, pot handles turned inwards, and the kettle and its cord pushed back from the edge.
  • Stairs and doors: a safety gate at the top and bottom of the stairs, and door stoppers to prevent pinched fingers.
  • Windows and balcony: window locks or restrictors, and no chairs or sofas placed under windows.
  • Bathroom: never leave your child alone in water, even for a moment; empty the tub after bathing and use a non-slip mat.

What to check before buying

  1. Stair gate: measure the opening first. For the top of the stairs, a screw-fitted gate is generally preferred over pressure-fit only.
  2. Cabinet locks: adhesive locks are easier to fit; check reviews for how well they stick to your surface type.
  3. Socket covers: pick ones that fit the sockets in your home and aren't small enough to be swallowed.
  4. Corner guards: strong adhesive that doesn't peel off easily — loose small pieces are a choking hazard themselves.

FAQ

When should I start childproofing?

Ideally before your baby starts crawling, in the first months — babies move faster than we expect. Review the house again as they grow and can reach higher.

Is a pressure-fit gate enough at the top of the stairs?

Pressure-fit gates are better suited between rooms or at the bottom of the stairs. At the top, a screw-fitted gate is usually recommended; always follow the manufacturer's instructions.

What matters most on a tight budget?

Start by anchoring heavy furniture and the TV and moving medicines and cleaning products to a high locked place, then add socket covers and locks on low cabinets.
