Best Travel Stroller: How to Choose a Light Stroller for Flying

A big everyday stroller is hard work at the airport and hotel. A good travel stroller is light, folds quickly and takes little space in the car or plane. There's no single "best" stroller for everyone; the best one fits your child's age and the way you travel. These points help you choose with confidence.

5 things that matter in a travel stroller

  1. Weight: the lighter it is, the easier stairs and airports are. Compare the weight listed on the product page.
  2. Folded size: some strollers fold very small, and some airlines allow them in the cabin depending on size — check with your airline before you fly.
  3. One-hand fold: very useful when your child is on your other arm.
  4. Recline and a big canopy: important for naps on the go and for summer sun.
  5. Age and weight limits: read the limits printed on the stroller, especially for newborns.

Airport and plane tips

  • Ask your airline in advance: is the stroller handed over at the plane door or checked with the luggage?
  • A stroller travel bag reduces scratches and dirt in the hold.
  • Practise folding and unfolding at home a few times before travel day.
  • Hang a small bag with nappies, wipes and snacks within easy reach.

⚠️ Always follow the safety, age and weight guidance printed on the stroller and use the harness every time.

FAQ

Can I take my child's stroller on the plane?

It depends on the airline and the stroller's folded size. Many airlines let you hand it over at the plane door; check your airline's website before booking.

Is a travel stroller suitable for newborns?

Only some are — if the seat reclines enough or it accepts a carrycot or car seat. Check the age range on the product page.

Do I need a travel stroller if I already have a regular one?

If you travel more than once a year or have a small car, a light stroller saves a lot of effort. For rare trips your current stroller may be enough.
