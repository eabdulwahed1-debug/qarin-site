How to choose a home blood pressure monitor

A blood pressure monitor is one of the most useful gifts for older parents, but accuracy varies a lot between devices. This guide helps you pick one whose readings you can rely on.

Note: this guide only helps you choose a device; it is not medical advice. Discuss your readings and how to measure with your doctor, and take the device to your first visit so they can compare it with the clinic's.

1. Upper arm or wrist?

  • Upper-arm monitors are what most medical associations recommend for home use, because they're less affected by hand position.
  • Wrist monitors are smaller and portable, but readings depend on holding the wrist at heart level, so they need more care.

2. Cuff size matters

A cuff that's too small or too large for your arm can give inaccurate readings. Measure around the middle of your upper arm with a tape and choose a monitor whose cuff covers that size (many come with a wide-range cuff, and some offer a larger cuff).

3. Choose a "clinically validated" device

Some devices have passed accuracy tests to international standards. Independent online lists of validated devices exist, such as the international STRIDE BP list; check the model number there before buying.

4. Features that genuinely help older adults

  • A large screen with clear numbers.
  • One button to start measuring.
  • Memory to store readings, ideally for more than one user.
  • Irregular heartbeat indicator (an alert, not a diagnosis).
  • Optional: phone connectivity to share readings with family or the doctor.

5. Tips for more accurate readings

  • Sit quietly for 5 minutes before measuring, back supported and feet flat on the floor.
  • Place the cuff on bare skin, not over thick clothing.
  • Measure at the same times each day and record the readings.
